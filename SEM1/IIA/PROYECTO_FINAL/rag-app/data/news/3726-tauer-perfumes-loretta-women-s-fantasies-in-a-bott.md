# Tauer Perfumes Loretta: Women's Fantasies in a Bottle

Category: Niche Perfumery. Author: Sergey Borisov.

I got a sample of Loretta eau de parfum on the last day of Pitti Fragranze . After lunch, some exhibitors began to pack and I sat on a bench to make up my mind and check if I met everyone I wanted to talk to and smelled all I wished to smell. I realized that I had passed several times by Andy Tauer's booth and we had greeted each other, but I had never stopped to smell his new creation. He was already packing when I rushed to his stand. "Oh! We need to correct the old saying from 'the early bird catches the worm' to 'the later bird gets everything,'" said Andy, smiling and handing me a beautiful tester of Loretta. I had a chance to smell the fragrance only when I came back home. And you know what? This fragrance is so far from the style of Andy I had known before! I am used to his perfumes being bright, powerful and impressive in their sillage. Loretta is different. It wraps you in a gentle floriental cloud. I would put it in a row with Fracas Robert Piguet and Poison Christian Dior , as a perfume created for special occasions. The Tableau de Parfums collection is the result of a collaboration between the perfumer Andy Tauer and the indie filmmaker Brian Pera from Memphis. The film anthology Woman`s Picture consists of three episodes about three women and the perfumes that play a key role in their lives. Loretta is the second perfume created for the second episode of the film. The first one was Miriam (introduced in 2011), and the third and the last will be Ingrid—all three are named after the main characters. Brian Pera about his creation: "Through the stories and perfumes we remember some of the women in our lives and families, explore how perfume influences and infects memory, and in some way try to determine what a perfume is saying when it speaks to us. What does sadness and regret mean in a fragrance and a film? How is it expressed? What brings happiness, bittersweet or joyful? When you watch a film, or you smell a perfume, how is it speaking to you, and how is it that what one person hears or sees or smells is so different than the next?" Loretta is a very sensual perfume, based on white flowers. It was with good reason that innocent girls were once forbidden from smelling night flowers—to avoid the seductive dreams and wishes their smells could provoke. I would expand the ban to caramel and vanilla as well. Impressive tuberose, in the company of jasmine, orange blossom and rose, appears shyly and even chastely. You can smell vanilla ice cream and a velvety touch of nubuck together with the flowers. The seductive character of the fragrance grows with the development of its gourmand sweet notes. "Sensual" is the right word for it, and I must admit I haven't attributed this name to any other fragrance with so much pleasure. If it was a dessert, I bet many of you would ask for another piece. The main character, Loretta, is a poor girl who works in a motel and dreams about another life, sourced in a forgotten bottle of perfume.
