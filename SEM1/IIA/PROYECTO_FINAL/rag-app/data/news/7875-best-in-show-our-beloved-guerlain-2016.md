# Best in Show: Our Beloved Guerlain (2016)

Category: Best in Show. Author: Elena Vosnaki, John Biebel.

With 188 years of continuous, unstoppable industry, Guerlain needs no introduction. The most classic of French perfume houses with an encyclopedic database of fragrances , it holds a special place in most everyone's fragrance-loving heart. But apart from the awe inspired by its masterpieces, there are facets in the story of the house and its perfumes which still manage to surprise and to intrigue. Is it the debunking of the love story of Jicky intended for the girl love-interest of Aimé Guerlain? Is it the provocative admission of Jean Paul Guerlain's that he intended to include his mistress's intimate scents (and he insisted on very intimate) in all his creations? Is it the intervention of outside perfumers into the traditional throne of the Champs Elysées? It's all that and more. Everyone has a beloved Guerlain fragrance it seems and our editors cherry pick their own. GUERLAIN SHALIMAR Ode a la Vanille Sur la route de Madagascar Stefanie Jähn : Released in 1925 velvety Shalimar has been a bestselling milestone in perfume history, it marked the rise of vanilla and is the epitome of oriental fragrances. Guerlain's house perfumer Thierry Wasser honored both, the emblematic Oriental and its emblematic key to success, the vanilla, with the three Ode a la Vanille editions. Each "Ode“ features a different type of vanilla. When I smelled Shalimar Ode a la Vanille Sur la route de Madagascar for the very first time I liked it, but could not really appreciate it. Back in the day I despised any kind of vanilla, or anything that smelled even remotely naughty but only a year later, my personal preferences had changed a lot. I longed for those bright citruses that lift positively musty, velvet curtains and show off a boudoir with powdery iris, smoky leather, balmy woods and a buttery Madagascar vanilla on the center stage. It not only smelled but felt other worldly, reminiscent of the roaring twenties, decadence, uninhibeted appetite for life, sexy, yet elegant, and very self confident. "Vanilla is famous as an aphrodisiac and I think that this is true. My grandfather, Jacques Guerlain, taught me how to love vanilla as it adds something wonderfully erotic to a perfume. It turned Shalimar into an evening gown with an outrageously plunging neckline," said Jean-Paul Guerlain. Shalimar Ode a la Vanille Sur la route de Madagascar cuts that neckline even lower but manages to preserve an elegant attitude even while shamelessly winking at innocent passerbys. Honorable Mentions : Guerlain L'Instant Magic , Guerlain Vol de Nuit , Guerlain Vetiver , Guerlain Samsara Eau de Parfum GUERLAIN VETIVER John Biebel : One of the first perfumes I bought for myself as an adult was Guerlain’s Vetiver . It was recommended to me by a clerk at the now legendary Colonial Drug, formerly of Cambridge, Massachusetts – New England’s home to classic perfumes and hard-to-find fragrances. I knew of the Guerlain perfume house, but didn’t know just how extensive their catalog was, or how much of an impact they’d had on the development of perfume around the world. Unboxing the striped frosted glass bottle of Vetiver was something akin to magic, and I still recall the satisfying clink that I heard when placing the bottle on the tile shelf in the bathroom. Vetiver is the gold standard of vetiver perfumes; the effervescent, exciting, “alive” vetiver that manages to draw natural freshness out of this dark and mysterious grassy root. It does so with a broad bouquet of notes that reinforce its masculine, confident edge: Pipe tobacco, bergamot, oakmoss, pepper, nutmeg, leather, carnation, tonka bean, sandalwood, sage. The whole affair has a sunny, bubbly opening of citrus and spices. The bold and green vetiver root moves in quickly to make its debut, and it fades many hours later to a soft, powdery-woody trace that is the hallmark of a quintessential modern male scent that has a distinctly Guerlain elegance. Vetiver was developed in 1962, and is a testament to the longevity of Jean-Paul Guerlain’s vision as a perfumer. It smells as fresh and relevant today as it did when it premiered at the beginning of the turbulent 60’s; a stalwart and timeless testament to sublime, aromatic greatness. Honorable Mentions: Guerlain Spiritueuse Double Vanille , Guerlain Liu , Guerlain Derby for Men , Guerlain Sous le Vent GUERLAIN LA PETITE ROBE NOIRE Sofia Shang : Created by its new in-house perfumer Thierry Wasser as a more permanent, mass-market re-launch, Guerlain La Petite Robe Noire enjoys its popularity, and numerous flankers ever since its inception in 2012. Just like the original idea of a little black dress being a versatile, affordable piece to complete many women’s wardrobes, I can see La Petite Robe Noire (LPRN) as one of those scented little black dresses that one can effortlessly put on. At first encounter, this young LPRN might come off as another sweet, oriental, vanilla gourmand scent, because many of our beloved Guerlain masterpieces have already stood the test of time. Yet time will tell how this seemingly simple-minded sweet scent is more than skin deep. Scrumptious, jammy cherry and sweet licorice are balanced by a whiff of dry tea notes; while the powdery almond and vanilla are long in coming, yet wind everything up like a soothing dream. LPRN is like an elegant cocktail when you are in no mood for a serious wine. With a touch of Guerlain sophistication, LPRN casts its sweet spell. Honorable Mentions : Guerlain L'Instant Magic , Guerlain Insolence , Guerlain Spiritueuse Double Vanille , Guerlain Elixir Charnel Gourmand Coquin . GUERLAIN MITSOUKO Eau de Toilette Elena Vosnaki : When erotic authoress Anais Nin reminiscences about her relationship with Henry Miller's wife, June, it is a bottle of Guerlain's Mitsouko she is asked for as a gift by the statuesque yet destitute woman amidst sapphic caresses. When Séverine, the bourgeois heroine of the cinematic adaptation of Joseph Kessel's novel "Belle de Jour", sets out to work the afternoon as a prostitute, it is a bottle of Mitsouko that she accidentally smashes in the bathroom, immersed in her sadomasochistic reveries. When Jean Harlow's husband, Paul Bern, driven by impotence, took his own life, a mere short week after his wedding to the silver screen goddess, it is Mitsouko by Guerlain he was drenched with; her perfume. Originally meant for brunettes, Mitsouko took the gentle but poignant star-crossed lovers of a Japanese brunette and a Russian naval officer, to inspire women (and men) of all hair colors and ethnicities ever since. Always implicated in sex in a "screw in the brain" sort of way, Mitsouko, with its tender peach skin heart and troubling inky base , is not just Belle. She's Belle Toujours. Honorable Mentions (from current production): Les Parisiens Derby for men , Insolence , Encens Mythique d'Orient , Vetiver , Jicky . GUERLAIN VOL DE NUIT Raluca Kirschner : With a classic backbone structure that exudes an enigmatic fragility, Vol de Nuit managed to defy the passage of time and is perhaps the most nostalgic and beautiful fragrance I know from Guerlain. Sharing the same name Vol de Nuit (Night Flight) with the novel written by the famous Antoine de Saint-Exhupéry that appeared two years earlier in 1931, the scent was inspired by flight and innovation. The square Art Deco bottle shows a stylized propeller, while the scent inside mixes shades of galbanum, iris and daffodils forming a tender green, very powdery and sweet cloud that envelops the balmy and a tad oily nuances of resins from underneath. It gives the feeling of a golden dust littered over a vintage gramophone that brought people to swing back in those golden days. A gentle woody-oriental fragrance of a sweet melancholy for the ones with misty eyes who smile while looking back in the past…. Honorable Mentions: Shalimar ; Mon Precieux Nectar ; Mitsouko ; Cuir Beluga ; L' Instant ; Apres l' Ondee. GUERLAIN DERBY Sergey Borisov : I first met with Derby by Guerlain in the early 90s. It was a strange meeting. I did not really like any of the notes enough so much to buy it. In my head flashed something like "some old cologne." Only Derby and Heritage deserved from me the designation, "Maybe when I'm older." I just was not ready for Guerlain. I was too young and without the proper amount of living needed to appreciate them. Derby was ink and oxygen, metal flower and lightning, clouds and sand. Additionally, Derby was not necessarily a perfume for younger buyers. His dry restrained heart is made for real men - Jean-Paul Guerlain wanted to call it the Centurion (later the name of the ancient Roman honored in Coriolan). Spicy, dry and dusty, leather hides his character; there is enough of wormwood, flowers and spices in it to earn the motto «Barbare et Tres Civilisee» (barbaric and very civilized). Civilized often reminds of Equipage Hermes, and barbarous parts - the tobacco, tar, patchouli and oak moss - just enough to not look like a savage. And it was one of the last fragrances of Guerlain, issued without regard to the IFRA that shattered the original aroma upon reformualtion. Three vintage Derby bottles cost me dearly. And each following the previous bottle is more expensive: Vintage Derby Guerlain is an investment. Have time to invest: the older the vintage, the more expensive.
