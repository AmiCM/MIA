# The Look, the Name and the Scent: From the Forums Makes a Connection

Category: From the Forums. Author: John Biebel.

From the Forums highlights our Fragrantica readers and what they’re discussing in the Fragrantica Club. There you’ll find many discussion threads that range from polls to opinions to requests for help with selecting something new for a personal collection. In the Perfume Selection Tips for Women , Fragranica member Queen of Sheba is looking for a recommendation for a pink perfume – but something fairly specific. As she puts it: “Something 'pink', a little fruity, and fizzy/effervescent or musky if possible. I want there to be some or a little sweetness but not overly sugary.” And readers have come to her aid. What I sense is that pink is as much a state of mind as it is a color, as many of you have described. Some popular suggestions include Mauboussin’s Lovely à la Folie , tart with lemon, apple and rose, and Escada’s Especially Escada , effervescent with rose, ylang ylang, pear, water notes and musk ambrette. Reader Magdalena23 insists that two of Guerlain’s Aqua Allegorias fit the bill perfectly: Aqua Allegoria Flora Rosa and Aqua Allegoria Rosa Pop . Osandra came up with an impressive list including Pink Room Parfum No 1 , BCBG Max Azria for Women , and Al-Rehab Super Scent for Women . Some fall very much into the gourmand category, whereas others are far more light and pale, what are your thoughts? General Perfume Talk In the General Perfume Talk , a frequently thought about but not often discussed issue arose: How much does the name or packaging of a fragrance influence you ? The opinions are scattered all across the board: ScienceWitch begins the debate by describing the perfume whose bottle stole her heart: Estée Lauder’s Knowing . She says “The name! the name! Oh and that brilliant name is on the cap, in this sophisticated, minimalist packaging.” Mystica suggests some of her personal favorites, but gives her idea on what makes for a classic bottle, making mention of Christian Dior’s Poison and it’s flankers: There is mention of the disconnection some feel between a fragrance, a bottle design, and a name. For instance, there is diversity of opinion about Molinard’s Habitanita Eau de Parfum , but many agree that it’s a beautiful bottle. Magie admits that a pretty bottle can be a very nice thing, but keeping perfume in good condition overrules style: “I admit I'm a sucker for a pretty bottle, and I do display unique bottles even if I don't wear the perfume. These fragrances are few though, and the ones I wear all the time, I too put them in a dark cool closet.” Rotorwife has a diplomatic view: “I love a great presentation/bottle but I won't buy something if I don't really like the scent. If I'm on the fence about wanting the scent... the bottle will usually be the deciding factor on whether or not I end up buying it.” Vintages In our ever-popular Vintages forum, Sushkin asks an important but tricky question: What is the official universal definition of a vintage perfume? This thread began back in mid-September and has had a modest 11 responses, but they’ve been fascinating and thoughtful. Cybernoir talks at length about materials in perfumes, scarcity at different times historically that can affect a perfume’s composition, and tweaks in a formula that will also change its character: As grayspoole says it, she’s rather matter-of-fact: “As you can see, there is no 'official' or 'universal' definition of 'vintage perfume.' If you are discussing Shalimar, for example, there is a century of versions before the current one and all of them could be called vintage.” The conversation turns to the question of what one is seeking in a vintage. Is it rarity, as in finding something that there is simply so little of left in the world? Or is it the nuance, like a fine wine, of something which has aged in a suave and graceful manner? Also, many believe the quality of ingredients available to perfumers was of a different caliber before our modern age of constant reformulation and revisions. What are your thoughts on the definition of vintage: Is it about time, or about an ideal? Added to our Database Finally, we’ve added some new perfumes to our database.
