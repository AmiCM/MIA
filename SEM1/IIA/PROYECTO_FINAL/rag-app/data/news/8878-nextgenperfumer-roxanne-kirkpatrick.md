# #NextGenPerfumer: Roxanne Kirkpatrick

Category: Perfumers. Author: Eddie Bulliqi.

This series shines light on the future talent of the fragrance industry. True for any craft, what youth might lack in experience and wisdom it makes up for tenfold in enthusiasm, experimentation, and audacity. Take note, their ideas will shape the fragrances of the next generation. Look here for the beginnings of innovation. Each one is a #NextGenPerfumer. Today, we introduce … The Snapshot CV Name: Roxanne Kirkpatrick Current position and company: Perfumer at Givaudan Based (town or city): Paris Nationality: American Education / training: Givaudan Perfumery School Hobbies and interests: Traveling, playing sports, reading, finding new creative outlets like sewing and trapeze courses, taking adventures to new places, meeting new people, trying new restaurants, discovering luxury hotels, and spending time with my family and friends. Most important moment in career to date: Completing the Givaudan Perfumery School and joining our team of perfumers at Givaudan. The Statement I strongly believe that if you know what you want and you’re consistently dedicated, resilient, and creative, anything is possible. In my hometown of Salt Lake City, Utah, I trained as a speed skater competing across the United States and internationally. After years of intense training and commitment, I became the United States Junior Sprint Champion which led to an once-in-a-lifetime experience: competing in the Olympic trials. I learned the values of dedication and resilience at an early age, making it possible for me to achieve the goals I set on the ice rink, and that these values equally apply to achieving your overall ambitions in life. Athletics was once my world, giving me the opportunity to travel to beautiful countries like Holland, Canada, Austria, Germany, and Italy. When I discovered the world of perfumery, I set my goal to become a perfumer for Givaudan and was very persistent in getting through the door. Once accepted, I worked as a summer intern, then Perfumer’s Assistant, and later started formal Perfumery School training in 2012. As a young perfumer today, I am driven by the principles I acquired in my previous sporting life and hope that people will feel my passion and energy in every fragrance I create. The Interview EDDIE: What drives you to create perfume? ROXANNE: I love that I have the ability to create memories and emotions through a very unique medium. It’s a very powerful gift to give to the world, and I am grateful and honoured to have been taught the skills to develop new fragrances to send out to new generations. Discovery is also a very big motivator. I surprise myself sometimes when I make a fragrance that is so much better than anything I have made before. It feels like a mark of personal improvement and I am powered up to get through some of the inevitable lulls that all artists face. Fragrance launches and their influence are truly global and the pace has never been faster. I believe our generation is riding a wave we don’t even know we’re on. It is an incredibly exciting time to be in perfumery. EDDIE: Who has been your greatest influence so far? ROXANNE: I have been very lucky to have an enormous support system at Givaudan. Even when I started as an intern in New York, there were people like Olivier Gillotin who were willing to share their knowledge and teach me. I have been blessed with what feels like a family, pointing me in the right direction and coaching me from day one. I am grateful for all of them. Currently, I am working with Natalie Gracia-Cetto who is my mentor; she is a dream to collaborate with. I feel so lucky to be a part of such a diverse team in Paris. EDDIE: Are there any perfumers whom you greatly admire? ROXANNE: I have a huge list of perfumers whom I admire, but I think more importantly I have developed a new level of respect for all perfumers. It takes so much resilience, patience, discipline, dedication, and maximizing creativity within boundaries which you don’t see unless you’re living it. EDDIE: What fragrance(s) do you consider exemplary or exceptional for the craft? ROXANNE: For my 10th birthday, my aunt took me on my first trip to Paris. One of our many stops in the city of lights was Galeries Lafayette. Just a few steps into the store, and we were approached by the Thierry Mugler sales woman, with a periwinkle star-shaped bottle in her hand [ Angel ]. I remember wondering how they possibly managed to put caramel and chocolate inside a perfume bottle and make it this colour? I was so enchanted and gracious being given my very first bottle of perfume from my lovely aunt. Looking back now, I had no idea that this scent was going to be revolutionary – it is a global success and the entire gourmand genre has been shaped by it today. EDDIE: What type of fragrances do you wish you to create? ROXANNE: I hope to create the type of fragrances that push boundaries, and make waves. This is the way I try to think when approaching projects – how can I do this in a different and unexpected way? At Givaudan, we have been reinventing some of the classic naturals to highlight the best aspects and facets. By starting with and embellishing the foundations of natural materials we are opening up a whole new direction for something that has been so basic for so long. At Perfumery School we had the opportunity to travel to a few of the sources of these naturals such as cistus in Spain and citrus in Italy; it makes such a difference to be with the plants at their source. There is a bond created between a perfumer and the material that is very special and changes the way we approach our work. So I hope to create fragrances that help translate the emotion and spirit from the source all the way to the shelf. On the other hand, I also love a challenge! Recently, I worked on project to recreate scent memories of babies, baby vomit, the crown of a baby’s head, baby shampoo, etc. There were already a lot of musks that I associated with the crown of a baby, so that was the most fun to explore. The baby vomit was a nightmare. The lab was not pleased with me either - as you can imagine, a lot of isovaleric and butyric acid floating around isn’t pleasant. You can smell these scents in the insert of the latest issue of the olfactive magazine NEZ . EDDIE: What will be your contribution to innovation in fragrance? ROXANNE: At Givaudan, we often have new naturals coming into our palette of ingredients. I feel very connected to naturals in general, and when our wonderful team of people bring us new naturals to smell, it’s like they’re bringing us a whole new adventure to discover. I go through phases; right now I am working a lot with citrus and immortelle. Also, with an upcoming trip to India, I am diving deeper into jasmine sambac and sandalwood, purely for the resonance it has there. Traveling usually sparks the phase of my next natural material love affair. I enjoy learning about the history of naturals, and the other properties they possess outside of their olfactive aspects. Working with naturals is emotional for me; you can feel the soul and depth of the scent with every breath. In practice, you can transform and embellish these oils in your formulas to create progressive accords. This is where I feel a strong emotional connection and passion for creation. I constantly ask myself, how can I share this with the world? EDDIE: Have you yet found a signature style or a genre that comes easily to you? Are there any fragrance types that are still quite a challenge for you? ROXANNE: I am a young perfumer, so every day I question what my style is. I strive to cultivate and nurture my formulas to become closer to what I envisioned in my mind when beginning the scent’s design. I am still evolving and growing. I hope I can answer that question again in a few years! EDDIE: Your craziest idea about the future of the industry? ROXANNE : I think we still have a lot of space to grow into the tech world. My husband and I think of crazy ideas all the time. He is a gamer, musician, and engineer, so we like to brainstorm ways that our industries will collaborate in the future. It’s not obvious, but one of these days someone will come up with something outrageous. For example, what if we could download our dreams into virtual reality and share them on social media? Scent is such a powerful sense and I could see it entirely revolutionizing the Virtual Reality industry to include olfactive sensors emitting scents based on moods, experiences, and scenarios. Imagine recreating a dream in VR and then adding either enhancing natural notes, or making them completely abstract to magnify the absurdity. Somehow I think fragrance, or at least the way olfaction works, will play an important role in this experience! EDDIE: What’s the next big thing? ROXANNE: I hope that our industry as a whole will start taking bigger risks. I hope we can invent a new strategy for the future, and change our approach to creativity and business based off of love. EDDIE: Where would you like to be in 10 years? ROXANNE: All I know is that I’d like to be breaking the mould. Exactly how, where, and when, I’m not sure.
